# How to use the program

## Underlined text = video demonstration

In the original PDFs, all the underlined text links to a video demonstration on Andrii's YouTube channel — just click to watch. In this wiki, those same videos are embedded directly: the [lesson library](/lessons) covers the full curriculum, and each plan has a video demonstrations page ([Basic](/basic-plan/videos), [Competitive](/competitive-plan/videos)) collecting every drill demo in order of appearance.

## Two plans in the package

- **Beginner training** — for those just getting started or with basic experience.
- **Competitive training** — for those who know the fundamentals and are ready to accelerate their progress. This plan follows an old-school Ukrainian boxing style, proven to develop high-level fighters.

## Eight workouts per week

5 boxing + 3 conditioning for maximum performance improvement. If you can't complete all sessions, you can adjust the schedule to fit your needs while maintaining consistency.

## Strength & conditioning

The package includes weightlifting plans (one shared by the two plans and a different one for the old-school plan), running workouts, and a circuit workout. Pay attention to proper form and load management to avoid injuries and excessive fatigue.

## Demonstrations

During demonstrations, Andrii avoids targeting his partner's head because his partner has visual impairments. If both you and your partner are in good health, it's acceptable to aim for the head. Keep in mind, this doesn't imply hitting hard or treating each other as opponents — it's a practice session meant for learning and skill improvement.

## Notation: n1 and n2

In the drill notation, **n1** refers to person number 1 and **n2** to person number 2. Each person has a designated role for each round, and you switch roles to practice both sides.

**Switching roles** means you mimic your partner's actions, and vice versa. The change can occur either after each attack or halfway through the round, depending on the drill.

## Equipment and partners

The program comprises various shadow boxing, partner drills, and bag work exercises. If you don't have access to a training partner or punching bags, you can adapt the partner and bag work drills for shadow boxing.

::callout{kind="tip"}
Having a partner isn't crucial in this package — do the virtual sparrings and bag work. But it's always a good idea to bring a partner, for motivation and a more interactive experience.
::

## Technique first

If you find certain exercises challenging, take it slow, concentrate on refining your technique, and opt for simpler variations. This holds true for both the boxing drills and the strength and conditioning exercises.
